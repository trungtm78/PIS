# Virtual keys of the AI gateway (ST-LLM-002)

Module document: `Design/MACHVON_Design/01_Documents/05_modules/LLM.md` (F5, BR-LLM-09). Table `ai_virtual_key` (baseline, V002; `V1141` lets an alias be held by the ACTIVE key only). Operations: `createAiVirtualKey`, `rotateAiVirtualKey`, `revokeAiVirtualKey`, and the read `listAiVirtualKeys` (new in the contract, permission `llm.key.read`). Screen LLM-06. Pure rules and ports: `packages/kernel/src/llm-keys.ts`; store: `packages/db/src/llm-key-store.ts`; sweep job: `packages/db/src/llm-key-sweep-job.ts`; controller: `apps/api/src/modules/llm/key.controller.ts`.

## The secret is in the vault and nowhere else

A key is made at LiteLLM (admin API, internal network) and its secret is written to the vault under `secret://tenants/{tenant}/litellm/{key}`. The application keeps the **fingerprint** (the start and the end of the SHA-256 of the secret, like `3f9a1c2e…7a2c`), the reference to the secret and the reference to the key at LiteLLM. Of these, an answer, the list, an audit row, an event, a log line and a screen carry **the alias and the fingerprint only**: `secret_ref` and `litellm_key_ref` are not in any view.

- **Answers and logs.** The create answer is `id`, `key_alias`, `fingerprint`, state and version. A failure of LiteLLM or of the vault is `503 PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE` naming the service, and what is logged is the class of the failure, scrubbed (`scrubSecrets` takes out what looks like a key: `sk-…`, bearer tokens, long runs of key characters). The message of a key service is never logged or returned, because it can carry the key.
- **Nothing is left half-made.** The moment LiteLLM has made a key, the command registers its compensation with `scope.afterRollback` (the command interceptor runs it, last registered first, after the transaction was rolled back and its connection released, whatever made the command fail: the handler, the stored answer or the commit itself). The compensation stops the key and removes its secret; if that cannot be done at that moment either, the key is written down in a transaction of its own as a key that is due (`ROTATING`, reason `CREATION_ABORTED`, alias not held), and the sweep stops it and takes its secret out of the vault, trying again at every pass. Running it after the rollback means that a dependency that is down cannot hold the connections of the pool while compensations wait for a second one. When the commit fails to answer after the handler returned (the connection is lost before the acknowledgement), the interceptor reads the record of the command (it takes its row lock, which the command held for the whole of its transaction, so a commit that is still running is waited for and not taken for a rollback; the wait is bounded to 10 seconds): if it is COMPLETED the transaction went through and nothing is taken back (a retry with the same key replays the stored answer); if it cannot be read, the outcome is unknown and nothing is taken back either, because revoking a key that was committed is worse than leaving one that was not. Two creations of one alias at once make one key (`lockAlias`); the other is `409 KEY_ALIAS_DUPLICATE` and leaves no key at LiteLLM.

## States

`ACTIVE` → `ROTATING` → `REVOKED`, or `ACTIVE` → `REVOKED`; a revoked key does not come back (`409 KEY_STATE_INVALID`).

- **Create.** Alias (3–80 characters), scope (`TENANT`, `UNIT`, `PROJECT`, `SERVICE`), the models (each once; they must be the tenant's and `APPROVED`: `422 MODEL_NOT_APPROVED`), an optional budget and rates, and an end that is in the future and not more than 366 days away. An empty list of models is a key that reaches none.
- **Rotate** (`If-Match`). Another key is made with the same alias, models and limits and is `ACTIVE`; the old one becomes `ROTATING` and its end is moved to a day from now (or its own end, if sooner), at LiteLLM too. Both work for that day. The models must still be approved. A failure after the new key was made undoes it, and puts the end of the old key back at LiteLLM when it had been moved, so that the key which is `ACTIVE` again does not stop within the day.
- **Revoke** (`If-Match`, a reason of 10 to 500 characters). The key is stopped at LiteLLM **before** anything is recorded: a revoked key fails at LiteLLM at once, and if LiteLLM cannot be reached the key stays as it was (`503`). From the moment LiteLLM has stopped it, the command registers a recovery for the case that it does not commit (an audit write, the stored answer or the commit fails): after the rollback the key is recorded REVOKED, with the reason given, its audit row and its event, in a transaction of its own. The secret is removed from the vault; a vault that cannot let go of it does not undo a key that is already stopped: the row keeps `secret_removed_at` empty and the sweep asks the vault again at every pass until the secret is out.
- **Sweep** (`llm-key-sweep`, every 5 minutes, see `docs/runbooks/jobs.md`). A `ROTATING` key at the end of its last day is stopped at LiteLLM and recorded `REVOKED` (`ROTATION_COMPLETED`); an `ACTIVE` key that reached its expiry is recorded `REVOKED` (`EXPIRED`). A key is recorded only after LiteLLM stopped it; one that cannot be reached is taken again by the next pass. The pass also removes the secrets of revoked keys that the vault still holds.

The reason of a revocation is written by a person who may have the key in front of them: what looks like a key is taken out (`[REDACTED]`) before the reason is kept, answered or sent.

Every command writes an audit row (`llm.key_created`, `llm.key_rotated`, `llm.key_revoked`) and an event (`llm.key.created.v1`, `llm.key.rotated.v1`, `llm.key.revoked.v1`), with the alias and the fingerprint and without the secret or the references.

## The screen

**LLM-06**: the table of keys (alias, scope, models, end, fingerprint, state; a key being rotated says when it stops), the note that the secret is only in the vault, the rotate and revoke buttons (the revoke form asks for the reason and says that the key stops at once) and the create form (approved models only). No field, button or message can show a secret. Standard states; an empty list still offers to create the first key.

## Assumptions and limits

- **Not deployed here (DONE_PENDING_ENV).** LiteLLM, Langfuse and the vault are services of the environment. The application talks to them through `LiteLlmAdminPort` and `SecretVaultPort`; until the adapters of the real services are bound to `LLM_ADMIN` and `LLM_VAULT`, `MockLiteLlm` and `InMemoryVault` (in memory) stand in. The operability gate of `infra/components/litellm-langfuse.json` (AC-ST-LLM-002-3, UAT-NFR-OPERABILITY-01) needs a real restore test on a clean host, so the component stays `PLANNED`; the runbook (`docs/runbooks/infra/litellm-langfuse.md`) and the alert rules are in place and now cover the keys.
- A process that dies between making a key at LiteLLM and the end of the command (no code runs after it) can leave a key at LiteLLM that the application does not record; the runbook says how to find such keys by their alias. The end of the old key of a rotation that could not be put back at LiteLLM after a failed rotation is logged (by class) and is found by comparing the ends in LLM-06 with those at LiteLLM.
- The reads of the keys (`llm.key.read`) are the tenant's and are not in any role of the design yet; they are granted with the permission matrix of the roles.
- A key is bound to models, not to a unit or a project: `scope_kind` says what kind of caller it is for, and the caller's own scope is judged by the gateway (ST-LLM-005).
