# The emergency stop and the decisions of people (ST-LLM-006)

Module document: `Design/MACHVON_Design/01_Documents/05_modules/LLM.md` (F7, F8, BR-LLM-11, BR-LLM-12). Tables `ai_kill_switch` and `ai_decision_record` (baseline V002, guards in `V1146`). Operations: `engageAiKillSwitch` and `releaseAiKillSwitch` (`llm.emergency`), `recordAiDecision` (`ai.decide`), and two reads new in the contract, `listAiKillSwitches` and `listAiDecisionRecords` (`llm.audit.read`). Screens LLM-18 (emergency stop) and LLM-20 (decision records). Pure rules: `packages/kernel/src/llm-gateway.ts` (`assertKillScope`, `killedBy`); stores: `packages/db/src/llm-emergency-store.ts` (`LlmEmergencyStore`, `AiDecisionStore`); controller: `apps/api/src/modules/llm/emergency.controller.ts`; the gateway reads the switches in `gateway.service.ts`.

## The emergency stop (F7, BR-LLM-12)

A switch is for one **scope**: the whole tenant (no reference), a provider or a model (its id in the registry; one that is not in the registry is `422`), a profile or a task (the name of the task; the profile and the task type are matched the same way). One switch of a scope is engaged at a time (`409 KILL_SWITCH_ALREADY_ENGAGED`, the whole tenant included: the baseline index did not see two switches with no reference, V1146 does). The reason has ten characters at least and is cleaned of anything that looks like a secret.

Engaging a switch, in the transaction of the command:
- **New requests** are refused at the door of the gateway with `503 AI_KILL_SWITCH_ENGAGED` (kept as `REJECTED_POLICY`, with its audit, like any refused request) when the switch is of the tenant or of their task. A switch of a provider or a model only takes those candidates out of the plan: the request goes over the next route, and is refused with the same `503` when nothing is left and a switch is the reason. Postgres is read at every request, and again before every attempt and after every answer, so the stop takes effect with the commit of the switch (the five seconds of BR-LLM-12 are an upper bound for the cancelling, which `llm_kill_switch_latency_ms` measures; the alert `LLM_KILL_SWITCH_SLOW` fires above 5,000 ms). There is no cache between the gateway and Postgres in R1: the table has a handful of rows and the Redis cache of the design is an optimisation that is not needed to meet the bound.
- **Requests in flight** that the scope covers are counted in `affected_requests`: those that wait (`ADMITTED`, `RESERVED`) are cancelled (`CANCELLED`, `AI_KILL_SWITCH_ENGAGED`) and their hold is given back; those that were sent (`DISPATCHED`) are marked `killed_pending` in the same transaction, so that their answer is thrown away when it comes; the switch is committed, and in force, at once, and only after the commit are the providers asked to cancel (`AiGatewayPort.cancel`): a request whose provider could is then `CANCELLED` and its hold given back, and one whose provider cannot stays marked. A request that the gateway is running in its own transaction is not locked by the switch (`for update skip locked`): it meets the switch itself, after its answer, throws the answer away, settles what was paid for and ends `CANCELLED`.
- The audit row (`llm.kill_switch_engaged`) and the event (`llm.kill_switch.engaged.v1`) say the scope, the reason and the counts.

**Release** (`releaseAiKillSwitch`, versioned) needs a reason of ten characters and **another person than the one who engaged it** (`403 SELF_APPROVAL_DENIED`; the database holds it too, `ai_kill_switch_release_chk`). A request that a switch overlapped is stopped even if the switch was released before the model answered (a switch is in force until its release, and the gateway looks at the switches released since the request began). A switch that was released does not engage again (a new one is made), and its scope, reason and engager do not change (V1146 trigger).

## The decisions of people (F8, BR-LLM-11)

`recordAiDecision` records what a person decided about the answer of a request: the module, the kind and the id of the subject, the kind of action (`WRITE`, `MONEY`, `DECISION`, `PUBLISH`), the decision (`ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_CHANGE`, `REJECTED`) and a rationale of twenty characters. The request must have been answered (`COMPLETED`, else `409`) and `ai_output_sha256` must be the hash the request gave (`422 AI_OUTPUT_HASH_MISMATCH`). The audit row and the event carry what was decided and about what, not the words of the person.

A command that writes, moves money, decides or publishes with an answer of the AI calls `AiDecisionStore.require(tx, { aiRequestId, aiOutputSha256, subjectKind, subjectId, actionKind })` in its own transaction: the latest decision for that answer and that subject; none, or a rejection, is `422 AI_DECISION_REQUIRED`; a hash that is not the one of the request is `422 AI_OUTPUT_HASH_MISMATCH`. After it has acted it calls `markApplied(tx, id, operationId, resourceVersion)`, once (`409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` for a second time). The table is written once: the database refuses to change a record, except for those two columns when they are empty, and to delete one.

## Screens

**LLM-18** lists the switches (scope and reference, reason, who engaged it and when, how many requests it stopped, state, and who released it with what reason), says whether anything is engaged, releases a switch (a reason of ten characters; the refusal when the person is the one who engaged it is shown) and engages one (the scope, the reference where there is one, the reason, and a confirmation that the person knows what it stops). **LLM-20** lists the records newest first, a page at a time, narrowed by module and decision, each with a link to how the answer was routed (LLM-05); nothing on it changes a record.

## Assumptions and limits

- **BID, PAY, DIS and OMNI do not call `AiDecisionStore.require` yet**: the port exists and is tested here, and adopting it in each module is the work of their owners (as the plan says). No command of those modules uses an answer of the AI today.
- A request that a provider has in flight is stopped at the next point where the gateway looks (after its answer); the request itself runs inside the transaction of its command until the adapter for a real provider splits it (see `docs/llm-gateway.md`), so a stop can only be as quick as the call.
- `affected_requests` counts the requests the stop found; one that is being run by the gateway at that moment and ends by itself because of the switch is not in the count.
- `llm.emergency`, `ai.decide` and `llm.audit.read` are tenant-wide: who holds them on a unit only is refused. The console reads the switches with `llm.audit.read`, so a role that engages stops needs both rights.
