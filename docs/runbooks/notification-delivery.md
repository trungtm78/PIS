# Runbook: notification delivery engine

Story: ST-NOT-002 (NOT 3.1, 3.6, BR-NOT-03, BR-NOT-04, BR-NOT-06, BR-NOT-13, BR-NOT-16). Screens NOT-11 (send queue), NOT-12 (journey of a send), NOT-13 (failed queue and replay).
Code: `packages/kernel/src/delivery.ts` (the rules), `packages/kernel/src/channel-port.ts` (the adapter port), `packages/db/src/delivery-store.ts` and `delivery-engine.ts` (the SQL),
`apps/api/src/notifications/delivery/` (dispatcher, adapters, webhook, controllers), migration `V1440__not1_delivery_engine.sql`.

## How a message goes out

1. An event reaches the notification consumer (ST-NOT-001). For each recipient it writes the notification with the channel plan of the workflow (`definition_json.channels`, in the order they are tried). `IN_APP` is delivered by the insert itself. For the external channels the consumer queues the first send (an attempt, `Queued`) on the first channel that has an Active channel account and an Active template; a channel without either is skipped and counted (`notification_channel_skipped_total`). The business command that raised the event has long returned: nothing in a command calls a provider (BR-NOT-16).
2. The `notification-dispatch` job (every 5 seconds, API process) serves each channel with sends due as its own bulkhead. For every due send it checks again that the recipient is still a member who may read what the message is about; if not, the attempt is `Suppressed` and the plan moves on. Otherwise one transaction takes the send up (`Queued` to `Sending`) and commits; the adapter is then called with no transaction open, with the idempotency key `{notification_id}:{channel}:{attempt_no}`; a second transaction records the answer.
3. The answer decides what follows:
   - accepted: `ProviderAccepted`, the circuit of the account closes; the provider's callbacks take it on to `Delivered` and `Read`.
   - refused and retryable (timeout before the request left, 408, 429, 5xx): the next send of the same channel is queued after 30 seconds, 2, 10 and 30 minutes (each with a random factor of 0.8 to 1.2, or the provider's `Retry-After` when longer), never at or after the expiry of the notification (it then ends `Expired` and nothing more is sent). A channel has at most five sends; after the fifth the next channel of the plan takes over with its own numbering.
   - refused and permanent (other 4xx, a wrong address): no retry; the next channel takes over at once.
   - answer lost (a timeout after the request left, an adapter error that is not classified, a process that died while calling): the send is `Unknown`. It is never sent again on its own (a second SMS is a second cost). A reconciliation task opens and `provider.result_uncertain` is written to the outbox.
   When no channel is left the notification is `Failed` (or `Unknown` when a send may have gone out, or `Suppressed` when every send was left out). A notification delivered in the application keeps `Delivered` whatever the external channels do.

## What the person decides before a send (ST-NOT-003: preferences, consent, quiet hours)

Story: ST-NOT-003 (NOT 3.3, BR-NOT-07, BR-NOT-08). Screens NOT-17 (consent history) and NOT-03 (preferences, staged for one contract operation). Code: `packages/kernel/src/notification-policy.ts` (the consent decision table, quiet hours), `packages/db/src/delivery-gate.ts`, `contact-consent-store.ts`, `notification-preference-store.ts`, migration `V1441__not1_preferences_consent.sql`.

The gate runs in the same transaction that takes a send up (`DeliveryEngine.claim`), after the notification is locked and before the circuit of the account is asked, in this order:

1. Preference: the person switched the channel off for the category of the event (`report` of `report.overdue`; `*` is every category) -> the attempt is `Suppressed` with `PREFERENCE_OFF` and the next channel of the plan takes over.
2. Consent: the decision table asks for one (everything but the inbox and the duty messages on a push; e-mail always) and the latest consent of the person for the channel and the purpose is not `Granted` -> `Suppressed` with `NO_CONSENT`. No adapter is called. A consent withdrawn at any moment before this transaction stops the send; the next check reads the database, there is no cache.
3. Quiet hours (push, SMS, Zalo, WhatsApp; never the inbox or e-mail): the instant falls in the hours of the person on their own wall clock (default 21:00-07:00 in Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh; none when they turned them off) -> the attempt stays `Queued` with `next_retry_at` at the end of the hours and the code `QUIET_HOURS` (NOT-11 shows it). A CRITICAL message whose workflow has `quietHoursOverride: true` goes at once and writes the audit row `notification.quiet_hours_override` (metric `quiet_hours_override_total`).

What to look at: an attempt `Suppressed` with `NO_CONSENT` is correct behaviour, not a failure (the person gave no consent or withdrew it; the history is on NOT-17 and in `contact_consent`). A `Queued` attempt with `QUIET_HOURS` leaves by itself when the hours end. To prove why a message was or was not sent, read `contact_consent` (append-only: a row is granted once and ended once, never deleted or rewritten) and the audit rows `consent.granted`, `consent.withdrawn`, `notification.preferences_updated` and `notification.quiet_hours_override`. The dispatcher result reports `sent`, `suppressed` and `deferred` for each pass.

## Honest adapter status

Every adapter has one of the four words of the connectors (`VERIFIED`, `PROPOSED`, `MOCK`, `MISSING`). No real e-mail, SMS or Zalo provider exists yet (D06), so by default there is no adapter: every channel is `MISSING` and its sends are `Suppressed` with the reason `ADAPTER_MISSING`, which is shown on NOT-12 and the failed queue. The in-memory e-mail adapter (`smtp-relay`, `MOCK`) is registered only where `MACHVON_ENVIRONMENT` names a non-production environment (`staging`, `ci`, `test`, `local`); a `MOCK` adapter in production, `dr` or an unnamed environment is refused with `MOCK_IN_PRODUCTION`, and a `PROPOSED` one never sends. To wire a real provider: implement `ChannelAdapter` (`packages/kernel/src/channel-port.ts`), register it in `defaultChannelAdapters` and give it a signing scheme for its callbacks.

## Provider callbacks (`POST /webhooks/channels/{provider}`)

Public and signed. The body is read raw (`rawBody`), the receiving channel account is found from the external account id inside it (the tenant in the payload is never trusted), and the signature is verified with the keys of that account before anything is stored: a missing or wrong signature is 401 `SIGNATURE_INVALID`, a timestamp more than 5 minutes from now 401 `REPLAY_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`, an unknown or disabled account or provider 404 `CHANNEL_ACCOUNT_UNKNOWN`. Headers: `X-Signature`, `X-Timestamp` (seconds), optional `Idempotency-Key` (the provider's event id). The keys come from `webhook_secret_ref` of the channel account, read from the environment variable `MACHVON_SECRET_<REFERENCE>` (`secret://production/mail-hook` is `MACHVON_SECRET_PRODUCTION_MAIL_HOOK`, value `keyId:secret`, several separated by commas while a provider rotates) until the vault exists; a reference that resolves to nothing refuses every callback. The raw body is kept by reference only (object storage, kind `webhook`); the inbox keeps its hash. The answer `{received, duplicate}` is sent after the event is persisted; applying it is the work of the inbox processor, right after and again from the `integration-inbox` job.

The status of a send only moves forward (`DELIVERY_STATUS_TABLE`): `SENDING`, `UNCERTAIN`, `ACCEPTED`, `DELIVERED`, `READ`, `FAILED`. A repeat or a late callback changes nothing and adds no history (counter `out_of_order_callbacks_total`); a failure reported after a delivery is a late callback; a delivery reported after a failure wins. A callback for a message id that is not known yet fails with 404 inside the processor and is tried again after 1, 5 and 30 minutes (the tenth failure parks it as `DEAD`, alert `INTEGRATION_INBOX_DEAD`). A provider read does not stand for the reading of the person: the notification stays `Delivered` (BR-NOT-05).

## Operator actions

- Replay (`post_notifications_id_replay`, permission `notification.replay`, `If-Match` with the version of the notification, body `Decision` with `decision: approve`): only for `Failed` or `Unknown`; 409 `NOTIFICATION_EXPIRED` after the expiry; 409 `RECONCILE_REQUIRED` while a send of unknown result is inside its 24 hours of reconciliation; 409 `NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` for anything else (and when the recipient is no longer a member). The new send continues the numbering of its channel; a channel that has used its five sends starts a new notification (`replay_of`, `replay_seq`, dedupe key of `replay:<id>:<n>`) so no channel is sent to more than five times per notification. Consent, access and expiry are checked again when the send is taken up.
- Cancel (`cancelNotification`, permission `notification.operate`, `If-Match`): the waiting sends are cancelled; a notification with nothing in flight or accepted becomes `Cancelled`; after that only `notification.cancel_requested` is audited and no retry or fallback follows.
- Both need the permission on the whole tenant (403 `SCOPE_DENIED` for a grant on a subtree). NOT-11 to NOT-13 read through `get_resources_delivery_attempt` (`?status=Queued,Sending`, `?notification_id=`, `?dlq=true`) and `get_resources_delivery_attempt_id`, which also returns the journey of the notification and the history of the attempt.

## Circuit breaker, reconciliation, metrics

- Circuit per channel account (`channel_account_circuit`): opens after 20 failed sends in a minute; sends wait (their attempts are not used up); after 2 minutes one send tests the provider; success closes it. Alert `CHANNEL_CIRCUIT_OPEN` after 10 minutes open.
- `notification-reconcile` (every 5 minutes): a send in `Sending` for more than 5 minutes becomes `Unknown` (`SEND_RESULT_LOST`); each `Unknown` send of the last 24 hours is asked about by its idempotency key: found moves it forward and closes the task, never seen becomes a failed send that the schedule repeats, cannot tell leaves it.
- Counters and gauges: `delivery_attempts_total{channel,outcome}`, `delivery_latency_seconds{channel}` (last value, creation to acceptance), `out_of_order_callbacks_total`, `notification_channel_skipped_total`, `notification_dlq_gauge`, `notification_unknown_oldest_seconds`. Alerts: `NOTIFICATION_DLQ_BACKLOG` (above 50), `NOTIFICATION_UNKNOWN_STALE` (above an hour), `CHANNEL_CIRCUIT_OPEN`. Nothing logs an address or a message body; the address of the recipient is read for the call and is not stored.
